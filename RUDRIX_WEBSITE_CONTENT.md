## 3. Brand Voice

**In one line:** an experienced person explaining things clearly to a business owner.

| Sounds like | Doesn't sound like |
|---|---|
| Confident, not arrogant | "World-class", "industry-leading" |
| Professional, not corporate | "Synergy", "stakeholders", "paradigm" |
| Technical, but easy to follow | A wall of acronyms |
| Friendly, not casual | Slang, jokes, exclamation marks |
| Experienced, not exaggerated | Superlatives and made-up numbers |
| Sales-oriented, not pushy | Fake urgency, "limited slots" |

**Rules we follow**

- Short and medium sentences. One idea each.
- Contractions: we're, we'll, don't, can't, you'll.
- Say what we actually do. "We use React and Next.js" beats "We leverage modern technologies."
- Explain the *why* for the reader. Every claim answers "so what does that mean for me?"
- Show experience through specifics, not by repeating "4+ years".
- Admit trade-offs. "A custom build isn't always the right answer" earns more trust than any slogan.

**Words we avoid unless there's no alternative:** innovative, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionary, world-class, next-generation, disruptive, synergy, digital transformation, unlock, empower, leverage, solutions (as a vague noun).

**Phrases we replace**

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| We leverage cutting-edge technologies | We use React, Next.js and Node.js, and we'll tell you why |
| Innovative solutions | A booking system your staff can learn in an afternoon |
| Transform your vision into reality | Tell us what you're trying to build. We'll help you plan it. |
| End-to-end digital transformation | Design, development and support from one team |

**Sample voice**

> We don't believe every business problem needs more software. Sometimes it needs a simpler solution. We start by understanding what's slowing your business down, then build technology around the problem, not the other way around.

### 4.2 Keyword usage rules

- **One primary keyword per page**, used in the H1 or first paragraph, the title tag and meta description, and a handful of natural mentions.
- **Secondary keywords** appear where the topic needs them. If a sentence reads worse with the keyword in it, the keyword goes.
- **Technology names** (React, Next.js, Node.js, MongoDB, Shopify, WordPress) appear in context: what we use them for and why.
- **No "best X in USA".** Location signals come from natural lines like "we work with startups and growing businesses across the USA, Canada, the UK and Australia."
- **Don't create near-duplicate pages** for keyword variants. One strong page per service.

We write code the next developer can understand, test the things that matter and build on structure that can grow. Products tend to live longer than anyone plans, so we build with that in mind.

Some clients hand us a whole product. Others want a designer, a developer or a second pair of eyes on an existing build. We fit around what you need: fixed-scope projects, ongoing work or a dedicated team.

Launch isn't the end. Real users show you what to improve, and we stay involved to help you do it. Many of the best decisions on a product happen in the months after release.

### Values

### FAQ (homepage selection; full set in the FAQ section)

**What does a software development agency do?**
A software development agency designs, builds and maintains software for other businesses. At Rudrix that covers custom software, web applications, SaaS products, mobile apps, eCommerce stores and UI/UX design. We also help with the earlier question of what's worth building, and the later work of keeping it running and improving it.

**How much does custom software development cost?**
It depends on scope, complexity and how many systems it needs to connect to. A focused internal tool costs far less than a multi-role SaaS platform. After a short conversation we can give you a realistic range and show what drives it, so you can choose where to spend. See the full answer in our FAQ.

**How long does a software project take?**
Small projects can take a few weeks. Larger products usually take a few months and keep evolving after launch. We'll give you a timeline after we understand the scope, and we'd rather give you an honest estimate than an optimistic one.

**Should I build an MVP first?**
Often, yes, especially if you're testing an idea or have a limited budget. An MVP isn't a smaller version of everything. It's the smallest useful product that answers your biggest question.

**Can you work with our existing team or product?**
Yes. We can join an existing team, take over a product, or review it and recommend what to fix first.

**H1:** MVP development: build the smallest useful version of your product

> An MVP shouldn't mean building a smaller version of everything. It means identifying the smallest useful product that can answer the biggest business questions.

### H2: Why most MVPs go wrong

> - The "minimum" quietly grows until it's a full product with a long timeline.
> - The team builds what's easy rather than what's riskiest to test.
> - There's no plan for how to learn from the launch.
> - It's designed so quickly that it isn't pleasant to use, and early users leave before they can give feedback.
>
> A good MVP is small, focused and well-made enough that people actually use it.

### H2: How we scope an MVP

**H3: Define the question.** What do you most need to learn? Will people pay? Will they come back? Can a certain workflow work?
**H3: Choose the core journey.** The one path that proves the idea, from first visit to the moment the user gets value.
**H3: Decide what waits.** We create a "later" list so ideas aren't lost, just scheduled.
**H3: Agree success measures.** Two or three signals you'll review after launch, set before we build.

### H2: What we deliver

> - **Product scope** — what's in, what's out and why
> - **User flows and UI/UX design** — clear, testable screens
> - **Development** — a working, deployable product
> - **Testing** — so first users don't hit obvious bugs
> - **Launch support** — deployment and basic analytics
> - **Feedback review** — a look at what happened and what to do next

### H2: How it works

> **Week one-ish: align.** A short scoping session to agree the question, journey and scope.
> **Design and prototype.** A clickable prototype you can show to potential users.
> **Build in short cycles.** Working software you can review regularly.
> **Launch.** Put it in front of real users.
> **Learn and iterate.** Decide together what to improve, extend or drop.
>
> We don't promise a fixed timeline before scoping. MVPs vary, but the discipline of small scope usually keeps them in weeks to a few months, not a year.

### H2: Who it's for

> Founders testing a new product idea, businesses validating an internal tool before rolling it out, and teams that need to show something real to investors or customers.

### H2: Why Rudrix for MVPs

> We'll push back on scope, because that's what protects your budget. We build the MVP on a structure that can grow, so a successful launch doesn't mean starting over.

### H2: FAQ

**Should I build an MVP first?** Usually yes if you're testing a new idea or have a limited budget. If you already have paying customers and a clear roadmap, you may be past that stage.
**Is an MVP just a prototype?** No. A prototype shows how something might work. An MVP is real software people can use.
**Can we build on the MVP later?** Yes. That's the intent. We structure it so features can be added without a rewrite.
**How much does an MVP cost?** It depends on the core journey and integrations. Because the scope is deliberately small, it's typically a fraction of a full product. We'll give you a range after scoping.

### H2: Working with a software agency

**What does a software development agency do?**
A software development agency designs, builds and maintains software for other businesses. At Rudrix that includes custom software, websites and web applications, SaaS products, mobile apps, eCommerce stores and UI/UX design. A good agency also helps with the earlier question (what's actually worth building?) and the later one (how do we keep it running and improving?). If you only need part of that, such as design or development, you can hire us for that part.

**How do we start a software development project?**
Send us a short description through the contact form. We'll read it, ask a few questions and arrange a call. After that we'll share a plain-language proposal covering scope, timeline and a cost range. If it feels right, we agree the first stage and start. If you're not sure what you need yet, that's fine: a short discovery phase is often the best first step.

**Can you work with international clients?**
Yes. We work with startups and businesses across the USA, Canada, the UK, Australia and other markets. We schedule calls to suit your time zone, communicate in writing so nothing gets lost, and agree clear milestones.

**Can you sign an NDA?**
Yes. If you want an NDA before sharing details, tell us and we'll sign one first. We treat client information as confidential either way.

### H2: Cost and timeline

**How much does custom software development cost?**
It depends on what you need built. The main cost drivers are the number of user roles, how complex the workflow is, how many other systems it must connect to, the amount of design work and any compliance needs. A focused internal tool costs far less than a multi-role platform. We don't think a price list is honest for custom work, but after a short conversation we can give you a realistic range, show what's driving it and suggest where to start smaller. `[Optionally add: "Projects typically start from $X" only if you're comfortable with it.]`

**How long does software development take?**
Small projects, like a marketing site or a simple internal tool, can take a few weeks. A larger web application or SaaS product usually takes a few months, and the product keeps evolving after launch. Timelines depend on scope, how quickly decisions are made and how many integrations are involved. We'd rather give you an honest range after scoping than an optimistic one now.

**Should I build an MVP first?**
Often, yes, especially if you're testing a new idea or working with a limited budget. An MVP isn't a smaller version of everything. It's the smallest useful product that answers your biggest question. If you already have paying customers and a clear roadmap, you might be past the MVP stage and can go straight to building the next release.
